        How to make a schedule in four steps

        1. Set the day. Choose the date and the hours your day runs. If you'd rather edit than start from a blank page, load the workday, student day or day at home example.
        2. Add your fixed blocks. Start with what already has a time: classes, meetings, meals, appointments, the school run. Give each one a start time and a length.
        3. Fill the gaps. Put your most important tasks into the free time that's left, one block each. Leave room for breaks and the time it takes to switch between things. If a new block runs into another, the schedule maker says so and offers the next free start time.
        4. Print or export it. Print the day or save it as a PDF, or add it to your calendar with the .ics file. Copy the link to open the same day in another browser.

        When a schedule maker beats a printable

        A printable schedule template is great when you plan with a pen. A schedule maker is faster when:

        • The plan changes while you make it. Moving a block takes seconds, and the others stay where they are. On paper you cross out and squeeze in.
        • You want it in your calendar. The .ics file puts every block in your calendar app with its start and end time, so you don't type the day in twice.
        • You need a clean copy. The printout has no crossings-out, and you can make the next version without starting over.
        • You're not sure it all fits. You see the whole day at once, and overlapping blocks are flagged before you commit to them.

        Frequently asked questions

        Is this schedule maker free?

        Yes. The schedule maker is free, with no signup and no account. It runs in your browser.

        Where is my schedule saved?

        In this browser only. Your day is kept in your browser's storage, so it is still there when you come back on the same device. Nothing you type is sent to a server.

        How do I open my schedule on another device?

        Select Copy link and open the link on the other device. The day, its blocks and their colors are stored in the link itself.

        What happens when two blocks overlap?

        The schedule maker tells you which block is in the way and offers the next free start time, so you can move the new block there in one click instead of stacking two things at once.

        Can I add my schedule to Google Calendar or Apple Calendar?

        Yes. Select Add to calendar (.ics) to download a calendar file. Open it to add the blocks to Apple Calendar or Outlook, or import it in Google Calendar's settings. Each block keeps its local start and end time.

        Can I make a weekly schedule?

        The schedule maker plans one day at a time. For a whole week on paper, use a printable weekly schedule template.

        Can I print my schedule?

        Yes. Select Print or save as PDF. The printout shows only your day, with its date, on one page.
