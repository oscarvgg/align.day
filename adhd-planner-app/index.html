ADHD Planner App: 6 Options for iPhone and iPad in 2026

Last updated: November 25, 2026. Align makes this page, and Align is one of the apps below. Facts about the other apps come from their own App Store listings and websites, read on that date. This page describes app features only. It isn't medical advice.

People with ADHD often look for a planner app that makes time easier to see, keeps the focus on one thing, or reminds them more than once. This page groups six iPhone and iPad planner apps by what they show you: a visual timeline, one task at a time, or reminders. For each one you get who it suits, where it runs, and one real limitation.

Visual timeline planners

Pick one of these if you want to see the whole day laid out as blocks of time, next to your events.

Align

Align puts your tasks on a visual timeline as time blocks, next to the events already in your iPhone calendars. You can give blocks colors and icons, repeat them, get reminders, and use overlap avoidance (in Align Plus), which shifts movable blocks out of the way when you drag or resize one. Locked blocks and read-only calendar events stay put, so an overlap with them can remain.

  • Suits: people who want to see how long each part of the day takes, as blocks on a timeline.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync between them.
  • Limitation: iPhone and iPad only. There's no pomodoro timer, habit tracking, Live Activities or Focus modes.

Tiimo

Tiimo shows a color-coded daily overview with drag-and-drop tasks, a to-do list for a brain dump with AI priority grouping, a focus timer with a countdown, widgets for your key tasks and timers, and sync with Apple Calendar and Reminders (App Store).

  • Suits: the listing calls it a neurodivergent-friendly planner and says it was co-designed with ADHD and autism experts.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The listing says a web version comes with Tiimo Pro.
  • Limitation: the free version has a basic planner, limited AI chats and one profile. Full features need Tiimo Pro.

Structured

Structured lays out tasks, events and routines on a drag-and-drop timeline, with color-coding and icons, an inbox to capture ideas, a pomodoro focus timer, Live Activities and interactive widgets (App Store, website).

  • Suits: its listing names people with ADHD, busy professionals and students. It also lists a dyslexia-friendly font among its accessibility features.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The website also offers a web app.
  • Limitation: connecting your calendars and Reminders, recurring tasks, custom notifications and Replan (which reschedules missed tasks) are in Structured Pro, a paid upgrade.

One task at a time

Pick one of these if a full day on screen is too much and you'd rather see only the thing you're doing now.

Llama Life

Llama Life puts one task in front of you at a time, with a countdown timer for every task. It adds up your list time and shows an estimated finishing time, and you can color tasks and add emoji (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want a timer on every task and a running estimate of when the list will be done.
  • Platforms: iPhone.
  • Limitation: after a 7-day free trial, it needs a monthly or annual subscription.

Routinery

Routinery breaks a routine into short steps of a few minutes, and each step runs on its own timer while a voice calls out what comes next. A reminder arrives at the time you set for each routine, and you can see the day as a list, a calendar or a timeline (App Store).

  • Suits: its listing names adults with ADHD who know the task but can't start, and people who lose track of time once they're absorbed in something.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: the free plan has two routines and shows ads. Premium, a subscription, lifts the routine cap.

Reminders that keep going

Pick this if your plan is fine but single notifications are easy to swipe away.

Due

Due re-sends a missed reminder at an interval you choose until you mark it done or reschedule it. By default it repeats five times, and you can raise that to ten; it keeps going beyond that only when you open Due or act on one of its notifications. It also has countdown timers, repeating reminders, and typed or spoken times such as "Dentist 3p Thu" (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want a missed reminder to come back several times before it goes quiet.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, with sync through iCloud or Dropbox.
  • Limitation: it's a paid app, and syncing with a Mac needs Due for Mac, sold separately.

Time management features to look for

Most "time management apps for ADHD" lists mix very different apps. These are the features that separate them:

  • Time you can see. A timeline shows how long each task takes and how much of the day is left. Align, Tiimo and Structured work this way.
  • A timer. Llama Life sets a countdown for every task, Routinery times each step of a routine, and Tiimo and Structured have a focus timer.
  • Reminders that repeat. Due re-sends a missed reminder at the interval you set, up to ten times. In the other apps, check how reminders work before you rely on them.
  • Less on screen. If a full day feels like too much, an app that shows one task or step at a time may suit you better than a timeline.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best ADHD planner app?

There isn't one answer, because people plan differently. Compare apps by what they show you. Align, Tiimo and Structured show the day as a visual timeline. Llama Life and Routinery show one task or step at a time with a timer. Due repeats a missed reminder until you act on it, five times by default and up to ten.

What should I look for in a time management app?

Look at what the app makes visible: whether you can see your day as blocks of time, whether it has timers or time estimates for tasks, and how its reminders work. Then try it on a few real days.

Are there free ADHD planner apps?

Align, Tiimo, Structured and Routinery are free to download from the App Store, each with optional in-app purchases. Llama Life is free to download with a 7-day trial, then a subscription. Due is a paid app.

Is there a printable ADHD planner?
