Best Planner App for iPhone and iPad in 2026

Last updated: November 25, 2026. Align makes this page, and Align is one of the apps below. Facts about the other apps come from their own App Store listings and websites, read on that date.

The best planner app is the one that matches how you already plan. Some people think in times on a clock, some in lists of tasks, and some in pages they write on. This page groups seven iPhone and iPad planner apps by those three styles, with who each one suits, where it runs, and one real limitation.

Timeline planners: plan the day on a clock

Pick one of these if you want every task to have a start time, so you can see what fits before the day starts.

Align

Align puts your tasks on a visual timeline as time blocks, next to the events already in your iPhone calendars. You can give blocks colors and icons, repeat them, get reminders, and use overlap avoidance (in Align Plus) so blocks don't land on top of each other.

  • Suits: people who want one visual plan for their own day.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync between them.
  • Limitation: iPhone and iPad only. There's no pomodoro timer, habit tracking, Live Activities or Focus modes. It shows your calendar events but doesn't replace your calendar app.

Structured

Structured lays out tasks, events and routines on a drag-and-drop timeline, with an inbox, subtasks and a pomodoro timer (App Store, website).

  • Suits: its developer names time-blockers, people with ADHD, professionals and students.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The website also offers a web app.
  • Limitation: calendar connection, recurring tasks and AI planning are in Structured Pro, a paid upgrade.

Tiimo

Tiimo is a color-coded visual planner with a to-do list, a focus timer, an AI co-planner, and sync with Apple Calendar and Reminders (App Store).

  • Suits: the listing says it's made for neurodivergent people, such as people with ADHD or autism, and for anyone who wants help with routines and focus.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The listing says a web version comes with Tiimo Pro.
  • Limitation: the free version has a basic planner, limited AI chats and one profile. Full features need Tiimo Pro.

Task-list planners: plan from a list

Pick one of these if you think in lists and projects, and only need dates and reminders on the tasks.

Things 3

Things 3 organizes to-dos into projects and areas, with Today and Upcoming lists, repeating tasks, reminders and calendar integration (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who want a personal task manager for their own work and life.
  • Platforms: iPhone and Apple Watch on this listing, plus separate Mac, iPad and Apple Vision Pro versions. Sync through Things Cloud is free.
  • Limitation: it's a paid app, and the Mac, iPad and Apple Vision Pro versions are sold separately.

Todoist

Todoist captures tasks quickly, by typing or by voice, and shows them in list, board or calendar views, with recurring tasks, subtasks, labels and priorities (App Store, website).

  • Suits: individuals and teams. The listing includes shared projects and task assignment.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch on the App Store. The listing says it syncs with Mac, browsers and other devices.
  • Limitation: the calendar layout isn't in the free Beginner plan; it comes with the paid Pro and Business plans (plans).

Notebook planners: plan on a page

Pick one of these if you like the feel of a paper planner and want to write or sketch your plan.

Goodnotes

Goodnotes is a notes app for handwritten and typed notes, with templates that include planners, plus PDF markup and audio recording (App Store, website).

  • Suits: students, professionals, and people who keep a planner or journal.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Mac. The website also lists Windows, Android and web.
  • Limitation: it's a notes app first, and planners come as page templates. The listing says some features are only available in select languages.

Planner Pro

Planner Pro combines events synced with your iOS calendars, tasks and projects, and daily notes you can sketch on, in day, week and month views (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want events, tasks and notes in one planner.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: projects, recurring tasks, week-view organizing, schedule export and an ad-free experience need the full version, a subscription.

Planner apps on iPad

All seven apps are listed for iPad, but they use the bigger screen differently:

  • For handwriting, a notebook app such as Goodnotes is the closest to a paper planner.
  • For planning on a timeline, Align, Structured and Tiimo show your day as a timeline on the larger screen.
  • For lists, Todoist runs on iPad from the same listing. Things 3 sells its iPad version separately.

If you use both an iPhone and an iPad, check that your app syncs between them. Align uses iCloud to keep your plan the same on both.

How to choose

  1. Start with how you plan today. If you already write times next to tasks, a timeline planner fits. If you keep a running list, start with a task app.
  2. Check your devices. Make sure the app runs on everything you use, especially if you also use Android or Windows.
  3. Try the free version for a week. Plan real days with it, then decide whether the paid upgrade is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best planner app for iPhone?

It depends on how you plan. For tasks placed at times on your day, try a timeline planner such as Align, Structured or Tiimo. For lists, Things 3 or Todoist fit better. For handwriting, a notebook app such as Goodnotes is closer to paper.

What is the best planner app for iPad?

Every app on this page is listed for iPad. Goodnotes suits people who like to write by hand. Align, Structured and Tiimo suit people who plan on a timeline. Things 3 sells its iPad version separately.

Is there a free planner app?

Align, Structured, Tiimo, Todoist, Goodnotes and Planner Pro are free to download, each with optional in-app purchases. Things 3 is a paid app.
