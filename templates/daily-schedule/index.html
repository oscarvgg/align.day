How to use a daily schedule template

Most schedules only show the plan. This one keeps a second column for the day you actually had, which is how you learn how long things really take.

  1. Write the plan in the morning. Fixed commitments first, then your priorities, then meals, breaks and travel.
  2. Leave a buffer. One open half hour in the morning and one in the afternoon absorbs most overruns.
  3. Fill in Actual as you go. A quick note at lunch and one in the evening is enough. Write what happened, not what should have.
  4. Compare the columns. Rows where the two differ show which tasks run long and when interruptions arrive.
  5. Adjust tomorrow. Give a task that ran over a longer slot, and move focused work away from the half hours that keep getting interrupted.
