What a visual schedule is

A visual schedule is a daily routine chart made of pictures: each step of the day is one square, in order, with a word under it. Parents and teachers use them so a child can see what happens now, what comes after it and what is left in the day, without reading a clock. The squares on this sheet are blank, so you can use the pictures and words a child already knows, from drawings and photos to symbols from a set you already use.

If you searched for an autism visual schedule, this sheet is a plain, blank format. If a teacher or therapist has suggested a particular way of building one, follow it and use the sheet as the paper.

Unlike the daily time blocking template, which plans a day by the clock in words, this sheet has no times at all: the day is a row of pictures read left to right.

How to make a visual schedule

  1. Make the cards. On page 2, draw or paste a picture on each card and write the word underneath, then cut along the dashed lines.
  2. Lay out the day. Put cards on the morning, afternoon and evening squares in the order they happen. You can also draw straight into the squares and skip the cards.
  3. Use First, Then, Next for the next three steps. Move the cards along as the day goes.
  4. Tick as you go. Tick the small box under each square when the step is done, or take the card off the sheet.
  5. Keep it where the routine happens, such as on the fridge, by the door or on a desk.
