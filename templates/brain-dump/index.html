Why sort a brain dump worksheet?

Writing everything down clears your head, but a long list on its own still asks for attention every time you look at it. Sorting is what turns it into decisions: a few things for today, the rest given a time, handed to someone, or dropped. When the table is full, every item has an answer.

The sort table is what sets this sheet apart from the ADHD planner, which has a small brain dump box and then plans one day with three picks and Now, Next and Later blocks.

How to do a brain dump

  1. Write the date, then write it all down in the dotted area. Tasks, errands, worries, ideas, half-thoughts. Don't sort, don't judge, and keep going until nothing new comes.
  2. Sort each item into one column, and cross it off up top as you move it. If an item is too big to sort, write its first step instead.
  3. Keep Do today short: three to five items. The rest goes to Schedule.
  4. Write the Who next to every Hand off item, then send the message or ask the question.
  5. Give each Schedule item a time. Write a day and time in its When field, then put it on that day's plan. The daily time blocking template has a row for every 15 minutes, so each item gets its own block.
