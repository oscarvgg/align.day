Why a goal setting worksheet needs a week row

Most goal setting templates stop at the deadline. The goal is written down, it's clear, and nothing in the week changes. The last two parts of this sheet close that gap: three first steps you can start this week, and the days and times the goal gets. A goal with hours in the week is a plan, not a wish.

That week row is the difference from the weekly planner, which plans a whole week of tasks. This page plans one goal, and the week row is only for its time.

How to write a SMART goal

  1. Write the goal in one sentence in the My goal box, the way you'd say it out loud.
  2. Answer the five prompts. Specific: what exactly, and what does done look like. Measurable: the number that tells you. Achievable: what you need and what could get in the way. Relevant: why now. Time-bound: the deadline and a checkpoint or two.
  3. List the first three steps. Make them small enough to start this week, and give each a date.
  4. Block the time. In the day boxes, write a time on the days the goal fits. Add up the hours. If the total looks too big for your week, the goal isn't achievable yet: change the goal or the deadline.
  5. Review on the date at the top. Tick the steps you did, write the next three, and keep the week row if it worked.
