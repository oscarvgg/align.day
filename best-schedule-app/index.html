Best Schedule App for Planning Your Day in 2026

Last updated: November 25, 2026. Align makes this page, and Align is one of the apps below. Facts about the other apps come from their own App Store listings and websites, read on that date.

"Schedule app" means two different things. One is an app for laying out your own day or week on a clock: when you work, rest, run errands and keep your routines. The other is an app for scheduling a team: publishing shifts, swapping them and tracking hours. This page covers both, in separate sections, so you can skip to the one you need. Align is only in the first group.

Daily schedule apps for your own day

These apps help you decide when each part of your day happens. Use one if you plan for yourself, not for a team.

Align

Align is a daily schedule app built on a visual timeline. You create time blocks for your tasks and routines, give them colors and icons, set them to repeat, and get reminders. Events from your iPhone calendars show on the same timeline, and overlap avoidance (in Align Plus) keeps blocks from landing on top of each other.

  • Suits: people who want to see their whole day, tasks and calendar events together, as one schedule.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync between them.
  • Limitation: iPhone and iPad only. There's no pomodoro timer, habit tracking, Live Activities or Focus modes, and it doesn't schedule other people.

Structured

Structured puts tasks, events and routines on a drag-and-drop timeline, with an inbox, subtasks, a pomodoro timer and widgets (App Store, website).

  • Suits: its developer names time-blockers, people with ADHD, professionals and students.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The website also offers a web app.
  • Limitation: calendar connection, recurring tasks and AI planning are in Structured Pro, a paid upgrade.

Tiimo

Tiimo is a color-coded visual planner with a drag-and-drop daily schedule, a to-do list, a focus timer and an AI co-planner, and it syncs with Apple Calendar and Reminders (App Store).

  • Suits: the listing says it's made for neurodivergent people, such as people with ADHD or autism, and for anyone who wants help with routines and focus.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The listing says a web version comes with Tiimo Pro.
  • Limitation: the free version has a basic planner, limited AI chats and one profile. Full features need Tiimo Pro.

Sorted³

Sorted³ puts calendar events and tasks on one timeline, which its website calls "hyper-scheduling", with an Auto Schedule feature and natural-language input (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who want to plan their whole day in one place. The website also has a section for students.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: a core set of features is free, and the rest needs PRO, which is sold separately for iOS and macOS.

Daily routine apps

A routine app is a narrower kind of schedule app: instead of planning a whole day, it walks you through the same steps every morning or evening.

Routinery

Routinery (listed as "Routine Planner, Habit Tracker") builds routines from short timed steps with voice prompts, with list, calendar and timeline views, streaks and stats (App Store, website).

  • Suits: the listing names adults with ADHD, shift workers, parents and anyone who wants a fixed daily order.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The website also links to an Android version.
  • Limitation: the free plan allows two routines and shows ads. Premium lifts the routine cap.

If you'd rather keep routines on the same plan as the rest of your day, a timeline app such as Align lets you set a block to repeat.

Team and shift scheduling apps

If you're a manager building a staff rota, you need a different kind of app. Align, Structured, Tiimo, Sorted³ and Routinery are personal planners and don't publish shifts to a team. These two do.

When I Work

When I Work lets managers build, auto-schedule and publish shifts, and lets staff trade shifts, request time off, clock in and message each other (App Store, website).

  • Suits: teams of hourly workers, in places such as restaurants, retail and healthcare.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad. The website also links to an Android app.
  • Limitation: it's built for employers and their teams, not for planning your own day. The app is free to download, but the scheduling service is a 14-day free trial, then a paid plan (pricing).

Homebase

Homebase builds work schedules, with a time clock, timesheets, payroll tools and team messaging (App Store, website).

  • Suits: small business owners and managers with hourly teams, and their employees.
  • Platforms: iPhone. The website also mentions Android and a web desktop app for admin work.
  • Limitation: it's an employee scheduling app for businesses, not for planning your own day. The App Store listing is for iPhone only.

How to choose a schedule app

  1. Decide whose time you're scheduling. Your own day points to a timeline or routine app. A team's shifts point to a shift scheduling app.
  2. Check that it shows your calendar. If your day already has meetings or appointments, choose an app that shows them next to your plan. Align shows events from the calendars on your iPhone.
  3. Plan a real week before paying. All seven apps are free to download, so try the free version, or the free trial for When I Work, on real days first.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best schedule app for planning my day?

For your own day, pick an app that puts tasks at times on a timeline, such as Align, Structured, Tiimo or Sorted³. If you mainly want to repeat the same steps every morning or evening, a routine app such as Routinery fits better.

What is the best app for scheduling employees or shifts?

Shift scheduling is a different job from planning your own day. When I Work and Homebase are built for managers who publish shifts to hourly teams. The personal planners on this page, Align included, don't do this.

Is there a free daily schedule app?

Every app on this page is free to download from the App Store. For the personal planners, check each listing for what the free version includes and what is paid. When I Work's scheduling service is a 14-day free trial, then a paid plan.
