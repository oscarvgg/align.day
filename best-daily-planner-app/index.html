Best Daily Planner App for iPhone and iPad in 2026

Last updated: November 25, 2026. Align makes this page, and Align is one of the apps below. Facts about the other apps come from their own App Store listings and websites, read on that date.

A to-do list holds everything you might do someday. A daily planner holds what you will do today, and usually when: tasks sit next to your meetings, so you can see whether the day fits before it starts. This page compares seven daily planner apps for iPhone and iPad that work one day at a time, with who each one suits, where it runs, and one real limitation.

What makes a daily planner app different

A good day planner app answers three questions a to-do list can't:

  1. What am I doing today? Only today's tasks are on the page, not the whole backlog.
  2. When? Tasks get a start time, or at least a place in the order of the day.
  3. Does it fit? Your calendar events show up next to your tasks, so you can see when the day is full.

The apps below answer those questions in different ways. Most show a timeline, one runs routines step by step, and two keep the day as a list or a page.

Timeline day planners

Pick one of these if you want every task on a clock, next to your events.

Align

Align puts your tasks on a visual timeline as time blocks, next to the events already in your iPhone calendars. You can give blocks colors and icons, repeat them, get reminders, and use overlap avoidance (in Align Plus), which shifts movable blocks out of the way when you drag or resize one. Locked blocks and read-only calendar events stay put, so an overlap with them can remain.

  • Suits: people who want to plan one day at a time as blocks of time.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync between them.
  • Limitation: iPhone and iPad only. There's no pomodoro timer, habit tracking, Live Activities or Focus modes.

Structured

Structured lays out tasks, events and routines on a drag-and-drop timeline, with an inbox for ideas, subtasks with notes, a pomodoro timer and Live Activities (App Store, website).

  • Suits: its listing names people with ADHD, busy professionals and students, and morning and evening routine planning.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The website also offers a web app.
  • Limitation: connecting your calendars and Reminders, recurring tasks and AI day planning are in Structured Pro, a paid upgrade.

Tiimo

Tiimo shows a color-coded daily overview with drag-and-drop tasks, a to-do list for a brain dump, a focus timer, an AI co-planner, and sync with Apple Calendar and Reminders (App Store).

  • Suits: the listing calls it a neurodivergent-friendly planner, for building routines and planning a busy week.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. The listing says a web version comes with Tiimo Pro.
  • Limitation: the free version has a basic planner, limited AI chats and one profile. Full features need Tiimo Pro.

Sorted³

Sorted³ puts calendar events and tasks on one timeline, with Auto Schedule to work out what fits in the day, notes and drawings on events and tasks, repeat rules and iCloud sync (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who want to schedule their whole day, events and tasks, from one place. The developer calls this "hyper-scheduling".
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: the app is free, but PRO is a one-time purchase after a 14-day trial, and the iOS and Mac versions of PRO are separate purchases.

Routine-first day planner

Pick this if most of your day is routines you want to run in order.

Routinery

Routinery turns a routine into short steps, each with its own timer, while a voice calls out what comes next. A reminder arrives at the time you set for each routine, and you can see the day as a list, a calendar or a timeline (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want morning routines, bedtime routines and study blocks to run on a timer.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: the free plan has two routines and shows ads. Premium, a subscription, lifts the routine cap.

List and page day planners

Pick one of these if you'd rather see the day as a list or a page than as a timeline.

Planner Pro

Planner Pro combines events synced with your iOS calendars, tasks and projects with subtasks, daily notes you can sketch on, and a focus timer, in day, week and month views (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want events, tasks and notes for each day in one planner.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: organizing in week view, projects, recurring tasks, schedule export and an ad-free experience need the full version, a subscription.

Things 3

Things 3 shows your to-dos and calendar events together in a Today list, with a separate This Evening section, reminders and repeating to-dos (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who look at their Today list each morning and pick what to do.
  • Platforms: iPhone and Apple Watch on this listing, plus separate Mac, iPad and Apple Vision Pro versions. Sync through Things Cloud is free.
  • Limitation: it's a paid app, and the Mac, iPad and Apple Vision Pro versions are sold separately.

How to choose a day planner app

  1. Decide how you want to see the day. If you think in times, pick a timeline. If you think in steps, try a routine app. If you think in lists, a Today list is enough.
  2. Check it shows your calendar. A daily plan that can't see your meetings will overbook you.
  3. Plan five real days with the free version. Then decide whether the paid upgrade is worth it.

If you'd rather start on paper, print a free daily planner template. If you want to plan your day in time blocks, see how a time blocking app works.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best daily planner app?

It depends on how you want to see your day. To give every task a start time, try a timeline planner such as Align, Structured, Tiimo or Sorted³. To run a routine step by step, try Routinery. If you want a Today list instead of a timeline, Things 3 fits, and Planner Pro adds daily notes to your events and tasks.

What is the difference between a daily planner app and a to-do list app?

A to-do list holds everything you might do. A daily planner holds what you will do today, usually with times next to your calendar events, so you can see whether it fits before the day starts.

Is there a free day planner app?

Align, Structured, Tiimo, Sorted³, Planner Pro and Routinery are free to download from the App Store, each with optional in-app purchases. Things 3 is a paid app.
