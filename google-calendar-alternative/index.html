Google Calendar Alternatives for Time Blocking on iPhone and iPad

Last updated: November 25, 2026. Align makes this page, and Align is one of the apps below. Facts about the other apps come from their own App Store listings, websites and help pages, read on that date.

Most people looking for a Google Calendar alternative want one of two things: a different calendar app for the same events, or a planner for time blocking their day next to those events. This page covers both on iPhone and iPad: seven apps with who each one suits, where it runs and one real limitation, then how to time block in Google Calendar itself and what to watch for.

Planners for time blocking next to your calendar

Pick one of these if your calendar is fine for meetings, but you want to plan the rest of the day in blocks.

Align

Align puts your tasks on a visual timeline as time blocks, next to the events already in your iPhone calendars. Add your Google account to your iPhone's calendars, then turn on its calendars in Align under Settings → Calendars, and its events show up next to your blocks. You can give blocks colors and icons, repeat them, get reminders, and use overlap avoidance (in Align Plus), which shifts movable blocks out of the way when you drag or resize one. Locked blocks and read-only calendar events stay put, so an overlap with them can remain.

  • Suits: people who keep their meetings in a calendar and want to block out the time around them.
  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad, with iCloud sync between them.
  • Limitation: iPhone and iPad only. There's no pomodoro timer, habit tracking, Live Activities or Focus modes. It's a day planner, not a full calendar app: it shows your calendar events but doesn't replace your calendar.

Structured

Structured lays out tasks, events and routines on a drag-and-drop timeline, with an inbox, subtasks, a pomodoro timer and Live Activities (App Store, website).

  • Suits: its listing names time blocking your work and study sessions, and morning and evening routines.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. The website also offers a web app.
  • Limitation: connecting your calendars and Reminders is in Structured Pro, a paid upgrade, along with recurring tasks and AI day planning.

Sorted³

Sorted³ puts your calendar events and tasks on one timeline, with Auto Schedule to work out what fits in the day, a Time Ruler for shifting things around, notes on events and tasks, and iCloud sync (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who want events and tasks scheduled together in one app. The developer calls this "hyper-scheduling".
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: the app is free, but PRO is a one-time purchase after a 14-day trial, and the iOS and Mac versions of PRO are separate purchases.

Calendar apps that replace Google Calendar

Pick one of these if you want a different calendar app. Each can show more than one calendar account.

Apple Calendar

Apple's Calendar app shows events from iCloud, Exchange, Google and other accounts together, with color-coded calendars, alerts, reminders from the Reminders app next to your events, and shared iCloud calendars (App Store).

  • Suits: people on iPhone who want one calendar for all their accounts without another app.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: calendar sharing, as the listing describes it, is for iCloud calendars.

Fantastical

Fantastical is a calendar with natural-language event entry, day, week, month, quarter and year views, and tasks, including Todoist and Google Tasks (App Store, website).

  • Suits: people who add a lot of events and want to type them in plain language.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch on the App Store. The website also lists Mac and Windows, and says Android support is coming in 2027.
  • Limitation: custom event colors, event templates, calendar sets and scheduling with others need a Flexibits Premium subscription, after a 14-day free trial.

Calendars by Readdle

Calendars by Readdle has daily, weekly and monthly views, an agenda planner, tasks, natural-language entry and a habit tracker, and syncs with Google Calendar and iCloud (App Store, help page).

  • Suits: people who want to keep their Google Calendar account but use a different app on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: the free plan is a basic calendar. Tasks, recurring events, reminders, natural-language input, multiple accounts and use across devices are in the PRO plan.

Microsoft Outlook

Outlook puts your email accounts and calendars in one app, and lets you share your meeting availability and find times to meet. It works with Exchange, Office 365, Outlook.com, Gmail, Yahoo and iCloud (App Store).

  • Suits: people who want email and calendar in one app, especially with a work Microsoft account.
  • Platforms: iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.
  • Limitation: its Copilot features need a Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscription, or a business account with Copilot.

How to time block in Google Calendar

You don't have to switch apps to try time blocking. Google Calendar has what you need to start (App Store, focus time help):

  1. Create an event for each block. Use the day or week view and give each block a start and end time.
  2. Color-code the blocks. Google Calendar lets you color-code events, so you can tell deep work from meetings and errands at a glance.
  3. Add tasks next to your events. Google Calendar shows tasks alongside events, so you can keep the to-dos for each block in view.
  4. Use focus time if your account has it. With an eligible work or school account (Google Workspace Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Education or Nonprofits), a focus time event can mute Chat notifications and decline meetings. You can create one in the iPhone and iPad app.

Where time blocking in a calendar gets awkward

  • Focus time needs a Workspace account. On a personal Google account, a block is a regular event, the same as a meeting.
  • Teammates see your availability. Google Calendar lets teammates check your availability and layer calendars. Decide whether your blocks should look busy to them before you block out a whole day.
  • The day changes. When one block runs long, check how you'll move the rest of the day: whether your app shifts later blocks for you, or you drag each one. In Align Plus, overlap avoidance shifts movable blocks to make room when you drag or resize one; locked blocks and read-only events stay where they are.

How to choose

  1. Keep Google Calendar for shared meetings if your work or family runs on it. Apple Calendar, Fantastical, Calendars by Readdle and Align can all show its events.
  2. Add a planner if what you're missing is the plan for your own time. That's where Align, Structured and Sorted³ fit.
  3. Switch calendar apps if what you're missing is the calendar itself: different views, natural-language entry or email in the same app.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Google Calendar alternative?

If you want another calendar app, Apple Calendar, Fantastical, Calendars by Readdle and Microsoft Outlook can all show your existing accounts. If you want a planner for time blocking your day next to your calendar, try Align, Structured or Sorted³.

Can you time block in Google Calendar?

Yes. Create an event for each block, color-code the events, and add tasks alongside them. With an eligible Google Workspace work or school account, you can also create focus time events that mute Chat notifications and decline meetings.

Do I have to stop using Google Calendar to use these apps?

No. Apple Calendar can show Google accounts next to iCloud and Exchange, and Calendars by Readdle syncs with Google Calendar. Align shows events from the calendars on your iPhone, so after you add a Google account there, turn on its calendars in Align under Settings → Calendars to see them next to your time blocks.
